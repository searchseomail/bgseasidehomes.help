How to Transfer Utility Accounts (Partida) to Your Name After Receiving the Notary Act

NeonVortex
Topic Starter
Posted: 05.02.2025 04:36
I recently finalized the purchase of my property and finally received the notary act from my lawyer. Now I need to figure out the exact steps to transfer the electricity, water, and gas accounts (partida) into my own name without delays. What documents do I need to present to the utility companies, and can this process be started online or do I have to visit their local offices in person?

Replies (12)

StellarPulse
Replied: 23.03.2025 01:15
Congratulations on finally getting your notary act! For electricity and water, you generally need a copy of the title deed, your ID or passport, and the old customer number or a recent bill if the previous owner left one.
RetroWave
Replied: 25.03.2025 16:32
When I went through this process last year, I found that visiting the local EVN or water office in person was much faster than trying to do it online, especially since they need to verify your signature on the application forms.
IronPhoenix

Make sure you take meter readings on the exact day you sign the act. The previous owner should clear any outstanding balances before you submit the transfer request, otherwise, the utility company might reject the change.

Some utility providers allow you to start the application online through their customer portal, but you will usually still need to visit an office at least once to hand over physical copies of the notary act.
