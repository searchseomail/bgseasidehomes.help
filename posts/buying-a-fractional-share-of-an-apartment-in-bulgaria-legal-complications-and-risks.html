Buying a Fractional Share of an Apartment in Bulgaria: Legal Complications and Risks

BlueNova
Topic Starter
Posted: 07.02.2025 07:24
I am looking into purchasing a fractional share of a resort apartment in Sunny Beach, Bulgaria, at a very attractive price, but I am worried about the legal loopholes. How does Bulgarian property law handle co-ownership of real estate, especially when one of the other fractional owners refuses to sell or pay maintenance fees? Are there any hidden risks with title deeds or notary procedures that foreign buyers usually overlook in these types of shared ownership deals?

Sunny Beach fractional shares are notorious for becoming absolute legal nightmares. Stay far away from this.
BlazeWalker
Replied: 09.06.2025 04:51
Under Bulgarian property law, co-ownership is governed by the Ownership Act, and dissolving a co-ownership when someone refuses to cooperate requires a court partition sale, which takes years and eats up all your profit in legal fees.
SolarKnight
Replied: 25.06.2025 06:01
If another owner stops paying maintenance fees to the complex management, the management company can put a lien on the entire property, not just their share. You could end up paying for someone else's defaults.
MysticCoder
Replied: 30.06.2025 05:53
Foreign buyers often forget that every single co-owner must sign off on major decisions, renting, or selling. If one person goes off the grid or is being malicious, your asset is completely frozen.
ByteRunner
Replied: 13.09.2025 13:27
Notary procedures in Bulgaria are legally binding, but a notary only checks if the documents are in order for that specific transaction—they do not protect you from future interpersonal disputes between co-owners.
SolarKnight
Replied: 11.10.2025 00:33
Before doing anything rash, make sure you understand the total cost of ownership. If you plan to hold it long-term, read up on living conditions and winter heating costs because maintaining a resort flat year-round is rarely cheap.
RetroWave
Replied: 30.10.2025 14:17
Fractional ownership in Bulgaria is often marketed aggressively to retirees and foreigners who don't speak the language, exploiting loopholes in standard notary contracts.
