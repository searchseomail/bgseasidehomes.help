Remote Property Purchase in Bulgaria: Risks, Power of Attorney, and Owner Experiences

ShadowRunner
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.01.2025 00:21
I am currently living abroad and looking to buy an apartment in Sofia remotely using a Power of Attorney (PoA) given to a local lawyer. Has anyone here gone through this process recently, and what were the biggest legal or financial risks I should watch out for? I want to make sure the title deed transfer is completely secure without me having to fly in for the actual signing.

Replies (11)

BlazeWalker
Replied: 03.02.2025 01:07
I did this exact process last year while living in the UK. My biggest advice is to make sure your Power of Attorney is drafted by a reputable Bulgarian notary or your lawyer, and if you are signing it abroad, it must have an Apostille. My lawyer handled everything from the preliminary contract to the final Notary Act without me being there.
StellarPulse
Replied: 20.02.2025 07:04
One thing people often forget to budget for are the notary fees, local taxes, and agency commission which can add up quickly. You should check out this guide on hidden costs and taxes when buying real estate in Bulgaria to ensure you aren't caught off guard by extra expenses at closing.
SilentEcho
Replied: 22.04.2025 15:17
Make absolute sure your lawyer conducts a thorough title search before transferring any funds. You need to verify there are no mortgages, outstanding utility debts, or third-party claims on the apartment. Reading up on how to check a property in Bulgaria for debts, liens, and encumbrances before purchase is a very good starting point.
NeonPhantom
Replied: 20.05.2025 11:02
If you are buying off-plan (new construction), the risks multiply significantly. You need to be 100% sure about the construction stage, especially if you are relying on a remote lawyer. Make sure you understand the legal status by reviewing Bulgarian property acts explained: what is the difference between Act 14, 15, and 16 before committing any money.
IronPhoenix
Replied: 17.06.2025 08:39
Never send the purchase funds directly to the lawyer's personal account or the seller's account directly without a proper escrow arrangement or a safe bank transfer method stipulated in the preliminary contract. Always keep a strict paper trail for anti-money laundering compliance in Bulgaria.
SkylineDrifter
Replied: 09.08.2025 08:47
My experience with a remote purchase in Sofia was surprisingly smooth, but that was entirely thanks to finding an independent lawyer who didn't work for the real estate agency. Do not use the agency's recommended lawyer—always hire your own legal representation to protect your interests.
LunarTides
Replied: 14.08.2025 02:36
Make sure the PoA specifically grants the lawyer the right to sign the final Notary Act and receive/transfer funds on your behalf if necessary, but keep the scope restricted so they can't do anything unexpected. A good lawyer will provide a bilingual draft of the PoA so you understand every clause.
NeonPhantom
Replied: 04.11.2025 09:33
Bank transfers from abroad can sometimes trigger compliance checks with Bulgarian banks. Ensure your funds are coming from a declared source, because Bulgarian notaries and banks are very strict about proof of origin when foreign buyers purchase real estate.
StormBreaker
Replied: 11.12.2025 07:44
When I bought my apartment in Lozenets remotely, we used a video call during the final signing at the notary's office just for peace of mind, even though the lawyer had the legal power to sign via the PoA. Ask your lawyer if the notary can accommodate a live stream or recorded session.
EchoMatrix
Replied: 31.12.2025 15:55
Beware of unannounced utility debts left by the previous owner. Even with a clean title check, sometimes old heating (Toplofikatsiya) or electricity bills pop up after purchase. Ensure your lawyer includes a clause in the contract stating all past utility bills must be settled before the final deed.
QuantumJumper
Replied: 24.01.2026 11:03
Overall, it is a very standard procedure in Bulgaria since many foreign investors buy property this way. Just keep communication lines open with your lawyer via WhatsApp or email, and don't rush the preliminary contract review.
