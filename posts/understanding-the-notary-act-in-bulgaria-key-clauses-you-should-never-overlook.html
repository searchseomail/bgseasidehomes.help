Understanding the Notary Act in Bulgaria: Key Clauses You Should Never Overlook

QuantumJumper
Topic Starter
Posted: 18.01.2025 15:07
I am in the final stages of purchasing an apartment in Sofia and the seller's lawyer just sent over the draft Notary Act. Since this is my first time buying property in Bulgaria, I am feeling a bit overwhelmed by the dense legal terminology and standard boilerplates. Could anyone point out the critical clauses regarding defect liabilities, payment schedules, and encumbrances that I should double-check before signing?

Regarding encumbrances, make sure the Notary Act explicitly states that the apartment is free of any mortgages, liens, or third-party rights. You should also review this guide on how to check a property in Bulgaria for debts before you sign anything.

Payment schedules are usually tied to milestones in preliminary contracts, but the final Notary Act should clearly state that the full amount has been paid or detail the exact bank transfer references if paying on completion.

Don't forget to budget for the local taxes, notary fees, and agency commissions on top of the apartment price. Take a look at these hidden costs and taxes when buying real estate in Bulgaria so you aren't caught off guard at the last minute.

Standard Bulgarian Notary Acts often include a boilerplate clause about accepting the property 'as is' (in its current condition). If you are buying a newly built apartment, you need to ensure developer defect liabilities and warranties are clearly written in.

Make sure the clause regarding the transfer of possession (handing over the keys) has a specific deadline. You don't want the seller lingering in the apartment for weeks after you've paid in full.
PhantomPixel
Replied: 07.10.2025 08:23
Check the tax valuation clause (danatsenna otsenka). Bulgarian law requires the Notary Act to state a price that is not lower than the municipality's official tax valuation, even if your actual purchase price is higher.
ShadowRunner

Ask the notary specifically to read out loud any handwritten or custom-added clauses during the signing appointment. Standard boilerplates are fine, but custom text is where things get tricky.
