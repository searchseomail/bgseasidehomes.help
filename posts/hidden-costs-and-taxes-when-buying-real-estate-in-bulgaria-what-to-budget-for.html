Hidden Costs and Taxes When Buying Real Estate in Bulgaria: What to Budget For

AlphaProtocol
Topic Starter
Posted: 05.01.2025 11:53
I am currently looking to purchase an apartment in Sofia and trying to calculate my total startup budget beyond the actual listing price. What are the mandatory notary fees, local taxes, and agency commissions I should realistically expect to pay during the closing process? Are there any hidden maintenance fees or legal costs that foreign buyers often overlook?

When I bought my apartment in Sofia last year, I quickly realized the listing price is just the starting point. You should generally budget an extra 10% to cover all additional expenses.
CyberNomad
Replied: 22.01.2025 15:47
Agency commissions in Sofia usually run around 3% plus VAT, which catches a lot of buyers off guard. Make sure you clarify whether VAT is already included in the initial quote they give you.
StaticPulse
Replied: 31.01.2025 09:08
Notary fees and local municipal taxes depend on the property's tax valuation, not necessarily the purchase price, though they usually use the higher of the two. Expect roughly 3.5% to 4.5% total for these state and local fees.
ApexPredator
Replied: 03.04.2025 16:21
If you are navigating the market as a non-resident, I strongly recommend reading this Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner to avoid missing any crucial legal steps.
PhantomPixel
Replied: 30.04.2025 10:19
Don't forget to factor in the lawyer's fee. A good independent real estate lawyer will usually charge between 0.5% and 1% of the purchase price to review the preliminary contract and check for liens.

Replied: 29.05.2025 03:30
Speaking of liens, before handing over any deposit, you absolutely need to know How to Check a Property in Bulgaria for Debts, Liens, and Encumbrances Before Purchase. Unpaid utility bills or old mortgages from the seller can easily become your headache.
BlazeWalker

If you are looking at newly built properties in Sofia, be extremely careful with the construction stage. Understanding Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: What Is the Difference Between Act 14, 15, and 16? saved me from buying a money pit that lacked proper occupancy permits.
AlphaProtocol
Replied: 03.10.2025 05:10
Maintenance fees (often called 'tax v hod' or building management fees) are something people forget. In gated complexes in Sofia, these can easily run 50 to 150 euros per month, sometimes billed a year in advance.
QuantumSpark
Replied: 29.11.2025 01:13
Also, remember that utility re-registration at EVN, Sofiyska Voda, and Toplofikatsiya takes time and small administrative fees. It's a minor hassle, but it adds up when you're moving in.
EchoMatrix
Replied: 05.12.2025 07:02
Bank transfer fees for closing the deal can also be quite high if you are moving money from a foreign currency into Bulgarian Leva (BGN). Always check your bank's wire transfer and exchange rates beforehand.
EchoKnight
Replied: 17.01.2026 06:33
Sometimes sellers try to push the tax valuation lower in the official notary deed to save on closing costs, and pay the rest in cash. As a buyer, do not agree to this—if you ever sell, you'll face a massive capital gains tax.
SolarKnight
Replied: 23.01.2026 04:58
Overall, keep a liquid buffer of at least 7,000 to 10,000 euros on top of a standard 100,000 euro apartment price just to sleep peacefully through the closing process.
