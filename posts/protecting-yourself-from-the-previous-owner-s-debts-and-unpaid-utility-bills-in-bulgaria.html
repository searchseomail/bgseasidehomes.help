Protecting Yourself from the Previous Owner's Debts and Unpaid Utility Bills in Bulgaria

SilentEcho
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.01.2025 04:43
Hello everyone, I recently purchased an apartment in Sofia, but I'm worried that the previous owner left behind unpaid electricity, water, and heating bills. The notary transfer is complete, but the utility companies are still asking for the old balances and threatening to cut off the services. How can I legally prove that these debts belong to the former owner and protect myself from paying them? Are there specific clauses in the preliminary contract or notary act that shield buyers from this situation?

StormBreaker
Replied: 19.01.2025 14:49
This is a very common issue in Bulgaria, unfortunately. First of all, utility debts belong to the *person* who consumed the service, not to the property itself. The utility companies (like CEZ/Electrohold, Sofiiska Voda, Toplofikatsiya) often try to scare the new buyer into paying, but legally they have no right to transfer the old owner's personal debt to you.

When you did the notary transfer, did you sign a handover protocol (preremeno-predavaten protokol)? That document should explicitly list the utility meter numbers and the exact readings on the day of handover. That is your primary shield.
CosmicDrifter
Replied: 07.06.2025 02:40
Toplofikatsiya (heating) is notoriously aggressive with this. They tried the same thing with me when I bought my place in Sofia. Take your notary act (Notarien akt) and the handover protocol down to their customer service center and demand they open a new customer account in your name starting from zero.

Legally, under the Bulgarian Obligations and Contracts Act, debts are personal. Unless you explicitly agreed in the notary act to assume the previous owner's debts (which nobody ever does), you are not responsible for past consumption.
