Realistic Annual Running Costs of a One-Bedroom Apartment in Bulgaria

ZenithGamer
Topic Starter
Posted: 04.02.2025 06:08
Hey everyone, I'm currently looking into buying a small one-bedroom apartment in Bulgaria (likely in Sofia or along the coast) as a holiday home and potential rental. Aside from the initial purchase price, I want to get a realistic picture of the yearly maintenance expenses, including electricity, water, internet, and building maintenance fees. Could anyone sharing their actual experiences break down what the typical annual upkeep looks like for a property of this size? I just want to avoid any hidden surprises before making a final decision.

Replies (13)

StellarPulse
Replied: 11.02.2025 05:19
I own a 1-bedroom apartment in Sofia, and honestly, the annual running costs are quite manageable compared to Western Europe. For utilities (electricity and water) plus internet, I spend around 1,200 to 1,500 BGN (approx. €600-€750) per year since I'm not living there full-time.
FrostBiteX
Replied: 19.02.2025 13:51
Building maintenance fees (known as 'entrance fee' in standard block apartments) usually run between 10 to 30 BGN per month in Sofia. It covers the cleaning of common areas and the elevator. However, if you buy in a gated coastal complex, those fees can skyrocket.
EchoKnight
Replied: 01.04.2025 08:29
Speaking of coastal complexes, watch out for the maintenance fees! In places like Sunny Beach, some complexes charge €8 to €12 per square meter annually. For a 50sqm apartment, that's an extra €400 to €600 a year just for the pool and security, whether you use them or not. You should read up on apartments without maintenance fees in Bulgaria to see how to avoid these traps.
NebulaRider
Replied: 22.05.2025 15:08
Electricity is generally your biggest utility expense during winter if you use AC for heating. My winter bills in Sofia for a 1-bed flat hit about 120-150 BGN a month, but summer bills drop down to just 30-40 BGN. On average, budget about 800 BGN a year just for power.
IronPhoenix
Replied: 10.06.2025 09:15
Water is very cheap in Bulgaria compared to the rest of the EU. I rarely pay more than 20-25 BGN a month for a single person household. That adds up to less than 300 BGN annually.
SolarFlare99
Replied: 01.07.2025 04:57
Fast fiber-optic internet and digital TV bundles are amazing value here. I pay about 25 BGN per month (around €13) for gigabit internet and TV channels. That's roughly 300 BGN a year, flat.
CyberNomad
Replied: 01.08.2025 15:50
Don't forget the annual property tax and garbage collection fee! For a standard one-bedroom apartment with a tax valuation around 50,000 to 70,000 BGN, you will pay roughly 150 to 250 BGN combined per year, depending on the municipality.
ShadowRunner
Replied: 02.09.2025 12:25
If you are looking at the coast, location matters a lot for your overall budget and year-round infrastructure. You might want to check out this comparison on buying property in Varna vs Burgas to understand the local costs better.
AlphaProtocol
Replied: 18.09.2025 13:31
Total it all up for a standard Sofia residential block: Maintenance (200 BGN) + Property Tax/Garbage (200 BGN) + Utilities/Internet (1200 BGN) = around 1,600 BGN (€800) per year. It's really affordable.
CyberKnight
Replied: 28.09.2025 08:40
Just a heads-up on insurance—home insurance for a small one-bedroom apartment in Bulgaria is optional unless you have a mortgage, but it's very cheap. A good comprehensive policy costs around 80 to 150 BGN annually.
AlphaProtocol
Replied: 19.10.2025 09:01
If you bought one of those cheap properties in Bulgaria under €30,000, just keep in mind that older panel blocks might have higher heating bills in winter because of poor insulation, so factor that into your running costs.
ByteRunner
Replied: 06.01.2026 10:03
If you plan to rent it out, remember that you will need a professional property management company if you aren't in the country. They usually take 20% to 30% of the rental income, which isn't a direct utility cost, but definitely eats into your net annual upkeep/profit.
SolarKnight
Replied: 28.01.2026 05:30
Overall, aside from the initial purchase taxes and notary fees (which are about 3-4%), holding a small 1-bedroom flat here will barely make a dent in your wallet compared to UK or German property taxes and upkeep.
