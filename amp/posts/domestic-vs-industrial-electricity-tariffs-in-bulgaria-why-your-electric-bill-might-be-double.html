Domestic vs Industrial Electricity Tariffs in Bulgaria: Why Your Electric Bill Might Be Double

EchoKnight OP
08.02.2025 09:11
I recently set up a small workshop in my garage in Plovdiv, operating just a couple of standard power tools, and my electricity bill came back nearly double what I pay for my household usage. When I checked with EVN, they mentioned something about commercial or industrial tariff classifications, even though I am just an individual running a micro-business. Can anyone explain the legal and regulatory reasons behind this massive price discrepancy between domestic and industrial rates in Bulgaria? Also, is there any legitimate way to have a small studio or workshop categorized under residential rates?

It comes down to EU directives and national energy regulations set by EWRC (Energy and Water Regulatory Commission) in Bulgaria. Household electricity is heavily regulated and subsidized to protect consumers, whereas commercial activities—even micro-businesses—are billed at free-market rates which fluctuate wildly.

EVN is just following the law here. The tariff depends on the intended use of the property as stated in your title deed and municipal registration, not on how much power you actually consume. Running a business from a residential garage technically violates the property's primary status.

To answer your second question: legally changing a garage or outbuilding to residential is nearly impossible if you are actively using it for commercial production. The municipality and EVN require a proper architectural reclassification of the building, which involves fire safety and urban planning approvals.

Many expats and locals try to skirt this by running low-draw equipment on household meters, but if EVN inspectors catch wind of it—or if your consumption profile looks too industrial—they can slap you with back-taxes and heavy fines. It's really not worth the risk in Plovdiv.
