Bulgarian Property Acts Explained: What Is the Difference Between Act 14, 15, and 16?

HyperSonic OP
06.01.2025 03:34
I am currently looking to buy an apartment in Sofia, and the real estate agent keeps mentioning whether the building is at Act 14, 15, or 16. As a foreigner, I am quite confused about what legal rights and safety guarantees each of these stages actually provides to the buyer. Could someone break down the differences in plain English and advise which stage is the safest to purchase at?

It can definitely be confusing for foreigners! Basically, Act 14 means the rough construction and roof are done, Act 15 means the building is fully completed structurally but not yet officially inspected, and Act 16 is the final permission to use issued by the state. Buying at Act 16 is by far the safest because you can move in immediately and get a mortgage easily.

Act 14 is basically just the shell of the building. You get a distinct legal property, but it's still a construction site with no water or electricity meters hooked up properly. Only buy here if you are getting a massive discount and trust the developer completely.

Act 15 is when all construction work is officially finished and signed off by the contractors and developer, but the state commission hasn't approved it yet. It's safer than Act 14, but you still can't legally live there or get a standard bank loan.

Act 16 is the golden standard. It means the state has inspected the building, confirmed it complies with all safety codes, and issued the Permit for Use. Utility bills will be at residential rates, not commercial construction rates.
