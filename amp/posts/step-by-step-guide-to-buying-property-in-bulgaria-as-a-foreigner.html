Step-by-Step Guide to Buying Property in Bulgaria as a Foreigner

NeonSpectre OP
15.01.2025 08:54
I am a non-EU citizen looking to invest in a vacation home in Bulgaria, but I am completely overwhelmed by the legal process and restrictions on land ownership. Could someone break down the exact step-by-step procedure for purchasing property there as a foreigner? Specifically, I'd like to know about the necessity of setting up a local company, typical notary fees, and how to safely transfer funds across borders.

As a non-EU citizen, the biggest hurdle you will face is that you cannot own land directly in your own name due to Bulgarian constitutional restrictions. However, you can own the physical building or apartment structure itself without any issues.
SkylineDrifter
20.03.2025 02:31
To get around the land ownership restriction, most non-EU buyers set up a Bulgarian limited liability company (OOD). The company can then legally purchase both the property and the land it sits on. Setting up the company is relatively cheap and straightforward, usually costing around 300 to 500 euros in total.
AlphaProtocol
21.05.2025 01:54
Step one is definitely finding a reputable local real estate agent and an independent lawyer who specializes in foreign property acquisitions. Do not skip the lawyer—they will conduct the crucial due diligence on the title deeds to ensure there are no hidden debts or mortgages on the property.
QuantumSpark
27.05.2025 07:20
Once you find the right property, your lawyer will draft a preliminary contract. This document outlines the terms of the sale and usually requires you to pay a deposit, standardly 10 percent of the purchase price, to secure the property while the final paperwork is being processed.
MysticCoder
01.06.2025 15:40
Regarding company formation, you will need a registered business address in Bulgaria, a small amount of capital (usually a minimum of 2 Bulgarian levs, though people often put more), and a corporate bank account. Your lawyer can handle most of this power-of-attorney style if you aren't currently in the country.
CosmicDrifter
06.07.2025 05:23
When it comes to safely transferring funds across borders, international bank wire transfers (SWIFT/SEPA) are the standard method. Make sure your Bulgarian bank account is ready, and always keep the source-of-funds documentation handy because Bulgarian banks have strict anti-money laundering compliance checks.
NeonPhantom
20.07.2025 00:27
Don't forget about the additional closing costs! Aside from the purchase price, budget roughly 4 to 6 percent extra for notary fees, local council taxes, agency commissions, and legal fees. Notary fees are calculated on a sliding scale based on the property's declared tax value or purchase price, whichever is higher.

After the preliminary contract and the funds are arranged, the final transaction must be signed in front of a Bulgarian notary. If you cannot travel to Bulgaria for the closing, you can issue a specific power of attorney to your lawyer to sign on your behalf.

Once the notary signs off on the title deed, the notary's office automatically submits the deed to the Bulgarian Property Register (Registry Agency) to officially transfer ownership. This registration process usually takes about 2 to 5 working days.

Keep in mind that if you purchase the property through a Bulgarian company, you will have annual accounting and tax filing obligations, even if the vacation home just sits empty most of the year. Factor in a small yearly budget for a local accountant.

One quick tip regarding currency: Bulgaria is transitioning toward the Euro, but currently uses the Lev (BGN), which is pegged to the Euro at a fixed rate of 1.95583. Make sure your international transfers account for any currency conversion spreads your home country's bank might charge.
